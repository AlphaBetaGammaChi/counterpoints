title: "Rebuttal: 'It's natural'"
---

# Claim: "Wood smoke is natural — humans have always burned wood"

**Short answer:** Tobacco is natural too. "Natural" says nothing about
safety — and "we've always done it" is historically accurate precisely
*because* indoor smoke was one of the great killers of the pre-modern
world. Wood smoke contains the same classes of toxins as cigarette
smoke, and the WHO's cancer agency classifies indoor biomass smoke as
probably carcinogenic to humans.

## The evidence

**Chemically, it overlaps with tobacco smoke.**
The standard toxicological review finds wood smoke carries many of the
same toxin classes — fine particles, carbon monoxide, PAHs, aldehydes,
benzene, dioxins, free radicals — with comparable mechanisms of harm.
→ Naeher et al., *Inhal. Toxicol.* (2007),
[doi:10.1080/08958370600985875](https://doi.org/10.1080/08958370600985875)

**Dozens of hazardous compounds measured directly.**
Source characterisation of fireplace combustion identified C1–C29
organic compounds including methoxyphenols and PAHs — many classified
as hazardous air pollutants.
→ Schauer et al., *Environ. Sci. Technol.* (2001),
[doi:10.1021/es001331e](https://doi.org/10.1021/es001331e)

**Probably carcinogenic.**
IARC Monographs Vol. 95 (2010) classifies indoor emissions from
household combustion of biomass fuel (primarily wood) as **Group 2A —
probably carcinogenic to humans** (household coal smoke is Group 1).
→ [IARC Monograph 95](https://publications.iarc.fr/113)

**Causes the same damage as the worst urban PM — or worse.**
In human lung and immune cell lines, wood-smoke particles triggered
oxidative stress, DNA damage and inflammation on a par with, or
exceeding, traffic-derived particles.
→ Danielsen et al., *Chem. Res. Toxicol.* (2011),
[doi:10.1021/tx100407m](https://doi.org/10.1021/tx100407m)

**"Always done it" = always suffered it.**
WHO still attributes ~3.2 million deaths a year to household air
pollution — mostly cooking and heating with solid fuels like wood.
That is the legacy of humanity's oldest fuel.
→ [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/household-air-pollution-and-health)

## Steelman — where the claim has a point

Humans did evolve around fire, and outdoor, well-ventilated burning is
far less dangerous than enclosed hearths. The objection is to *enclosed
domestic burning in dense neighbourhoods* — where concentrations are
chronic and involuntary.

## Why it still fails

- The **appeal to nature is a fallacy**: arsenic, radon, asbestos,
  malaria and tobacco are all natural.
- The "tradition" is one of chronic disease: open hearths are a
  documented driver of respiratory illness wherever they remain in use.
- Ancestral exposure was mostly **in low-density settings** — not
  terraced streets where one chimney feeds a dozen gardens.

## Bottom line

"Natural and traditional" is a marketing line, not a health assessment.
The measured chemistry — PAHs, benzene, dioxins, formaldehyde, fine
particles — is what determines harm, and it reads like a tobacco
smoke inventory.
