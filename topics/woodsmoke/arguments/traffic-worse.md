title: "Rebuttal: 'Traffic is worse'"
---

# Claim: "Traffic and industry are far bigger pollution problems"

**Short answer:** They aren't. Domestic wood burning is the **largest
single source of primary PM2.5 in the UK** (29% vs road transport's 18%),
roughly **half of all anthropogenic PM2.5 in the EU**, and **21% of
Canada's PM2.5 — more than all transport combined**. And unlike traffic,
it's concentrated at breathing height in residential streets, on winter
evenings, where monitoring doesn't see it.

## The evidence

**UK — the largest source, still rising:**
Domestic burning supplied **29% of UK primary PM2.5 in 2022** (road
transport 18%, industrial processes 16.5%); ~three-quarters of it is
wood. It is the only major source whose emissions have *increased*
over the last 20 years — domestic wood burning PM2.5 rose 124% between
2011 and 2021.
→ [Defra/NAEI evidence to Environmental Audit Committee](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/163116/html/);
[Defra emissions statistics](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/emissions-of-air-pollutants/emissions-of-air-pollutants-in-the-uk-particulate-matter-pm10-and-pm25)

**EU — around half of anthropogenic PM2.5:**
Wood burning is the single largest source of PM2.5 in the European
Union (~51%).
→ Glojek et al., *Atmos. Chem. Phys.* (2022),
[doi:10.5194/acp-22-5577-2022](https://doi.org/10.5194/acp-22-5577-2022)

**Canada — more than all transport:**
Homes burning wood emit ~21% of Canada's PM2.5 vs ~12% from all on-
and off-road transport combined.
→ [Health Canada (2023)](https://publications.gc.ca/site/eng/9.917507/publication.html)

**Local apportionment is even more extreme:**

|---|---|---|
| Lycksele, N. Sweden — winter PM1 | up to **81%** | [Krecl 2008](https://doi.org/10.5194/acp-8-3639-2008) |
| Swiss urban/rural sites — black carbon | up to **~33%** (from <4% of energy use) | [Herich 2011](https://doi.org/10.5194/amt-4-1409-2011) |

| London — winter PM10 | measurable and growing | [Fuller 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.atmosenv.2013.12.037) |

## Steelman — where the claim has a point

Traffic also produces NO₂ and near-road exposure that wood smoke
doesn't, and *secondary* PM2.5 (formed in the atmosphere from gases)
changes the accounting — looking only at *primary* PM2.5 slightly
overstates wood's share of total ambient PM2.5 concentration.

## Why it still fails

- The comparison that matters is **PM2.5 emissions**, where wood
  burning is demonstrably the largest single UK/EU source — this is
  official inventory data, not campaign spin.
- **Trajectory:** every transport and industrial source is trending
  down under regulation; domestic wood burning is the *only* UK source
  still rising.
- **Exposure is what harms.** A motorway's PM2.5 is diluted across a
  corridor; a chimney's PM2.5 pools in the street, at head height, in
  inversion conditions, on the evenings everyone is home — and
  infiltrates living rooms.
- "Someone else is also bad" is **whataboutism**, not an argument for
  continuing to burn.

## Bottom line

Whatever traffic does or doesn't emit, wood burning is the largest
primary PM2.5 source in the UK and EU, is still growing, and deposits
its pollution exactly where people live and breathe. "But cars!"
distracts from the one source actively getting worse.
