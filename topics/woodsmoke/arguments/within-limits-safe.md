---
title: "Rebuttal: 'Within legal limits = safe'"
---

# Claim: "If it's within legal limits, it's safe — they'd ban it otherwise"

**Short answer:** There is no known safe level of PM2.5 — the WHO says so
explicitly — and harm is measurable well below current legal limits.
"Legal" reflects what regulation has caught up to, not what the science
says; the same logic once applied to tobacco and leaded petrol.

## The evidence

**WHO: no safe threshold.**
The 2021 WHO Air Quality Guidelines set PM2.5 at 5 µg/m³ annual /
15 µg/m³ 24-h — and explicitly state there is no evidence of a level
below which no damage occurs.
→ [WHO Global Air Quality Guidelines (2021)](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240034228)

**Harm continues below legal standards.**
Global mortality modelling finds risk from PM2.5 persists at
concentrations well inside current regulatory limits — ~8.9 million
premature deaths attributable in 2015 alone.
→ Burnett et al., *PNAS* (2018),
[doi:10.1073/pnas.1803222115](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1803222115)

**Wood-smoke communities show harm below limits.**
Adverse respiratory and cardiovascular effects are documented in modern
wood-burning communities even where legal limits are met.
→ Boman et al., *Scand. J. Work Environ. Health* (2003),
[doi:10.5271/sjweh.729](https://doi.org/10.5271/sjweh.729)

**Controlled exposure: hours, not years.**
Measurable airway and systemic responses follow just a few hours of
wood-smoke exposure in susceptible people.
→ Riddervold et al., *Part. Fibre Toxicol.* (2012),
[doi:10.1186/1743-8977-9-12](https://doi.org/10.1186/1743-8977-9-12)

**"Legal" is a lagging indicator.**
UK officialdom itself now names air pollution the largest environmental
threat to health and domestic burning the largest PM2.5 source —
the CMO's 2022 report is effectively an admission that regulation is
behind the evidence.
→ [Chief Medical Officer's Annual Report 2022](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/chief-medical-officers-annual-report-2022-air-pollution)

**The killer stat:** WHO attributes ~3.2 million deaths a year to
household air pollution — solid fuels, "within limits" nowhere.
→ [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/household-air-pollution-and-health)

## Steelman — where the claim has a point

Legal limits do reflect real evidence and have driven genuine
improvement; and an individual stove's contribution to a regional
average can be small. If you only ever read the regional compliance
map, the problem looks abstract.

## Why it still fails

- **Compliance is measured where smoke isn't.** Hotspots are
  hyper-local and unmonitored — "the city is within limits" says
  nothing about the street behind a row of chimneys at 7 pm in
  January (see [Su et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11869-011-0148-1);
  [DSAWSP](https://www.dsawsp.org/health/wood-smoke-is-pm)).
- **Averages hide peaks.** Wood smoke is episodic — legal annual
  means coexist with nightly 10–100× local peaks.
- History's verdict is consistent: tobacco, asbestos, leaded petrol,
  coal smoke — all "legal until proven lethal", all lethal long
  before the law moved.

## Bottom line

"There is no safe level of PM2.5" is not activist rhetoric — it is the
position of the WHO and the consistent finding of the epidemiology.
Legal limits are a compromise between evidence and feasibility, not a
safe/unsafe boundary — and for this particular source, they are set
where the monitors cannot even see the smoke.
