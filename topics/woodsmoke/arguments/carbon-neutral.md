title: "Rebuttal: 'Wood is carbon neutral'"
---

# Claim: "Wood is renewable, so burning it is carbon neutral"

**Short answer:** Burning wood releases *more* CO₂ per unit of heat up front
than coal. Any "carbon neutrality" depends on future tree regrowth repaying
the debt over decades — time the climate does not have — and that's before
counting black carbon and brown carbon, which make wood smoke an even worse
climate actor in the near term. When short-lived climate pollutants are
counted, wood stoves come out as the *least* climate-friendly way to heat
a home.

## The evidence

**The carbon debt is real and long.**
Dynamic lifecycle analysis shows replacing coal with wood *increases*
atmospheric CO₂ for decades: wood is less energy-dense and burns less
efficiently, so more carbon is emitted per unit of energy immediately,
while regrowth repayment is slow and uncertain.
→ Sterman, Booth & Kates, *Environ. Res. Lett.* (2018),
[doi:10.1088/1748-9326/aaa512](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/aaa512)

**Black carbon is a potent near-term warmer.**
Wood smoke is a major source of black carbon — ranked the second most
important human warming agent after CO₂ — which heats the atmosphere
directly and accelerates snow/ice melt when deposited.
→ Bond et al., *J. Geophys. Res.* (2013),
[doi:10.1002/jgrd.50171](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgrd.50171)

**Brown carbon adds warming on top.**
Nitrated phenols and other organics in wood smoke absorb sunlight —
a previously overlooked warming contribution measured directly in UK
winter air.
→ Mohr et al., *Environ. Sci. Technol.* (2013),
[doi:10.1021/es400683v](https://doi.org/10.1021/es400683v)

**Counting all pollutants flips the ranking.**
When black carbon and methane are included, Finnish residential wood
stoves were found to be the *least* climate-friendly heating option
assessed.
→ Savolahti et al., *Energy Policy* (2019),
[doi:10.1016/j.enpol.2019.06.045](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2019.06.045)

**Regulators are acting on this.**
California's air agency projects residential wood burning will become
the state's largest anthropogenic source of black carbon by 2030.
→ [CARB, Final SLCP Reduction Strategy (2020)](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/final_SLCP_strategy.pdf)

## Steelman — where the claim has a point

Managed-forest carbon cycles are real: a sustainably harvested forest
does re-sequester carbon, and industrial pellet boilers with modern
abatement are a different proposition from a domestic stove. "Carbon
neutral eventually" is not always false.

## Why it still fails

- **Timing is everything.** "Neutral in 50–100 years" is a debt, not
  neutrality. The IPCC horizon for halving emissions is *this decade*.
- **The debt is uncertain.** Repayment assumes the forest regrows and is
  never itself burned, cleared, or killed by drought/pests — the very
  pressures climate change is increasing.
- **The warming isn't just CO₂.** Black and brown carbon warm the
  atmosphere *now* and are not cancelled by regrowth accounting.
- **Per unit of heat, alternatives are simply better.** Gas emits less
  CO₂ *and* ~450× less PM2.5; heat pumps emit neither at the point of
  use.

## Bottom line

"Carbon neutral" is an accounting convention spread over a century, used
to market a fuel that emits more CO₂ upfront than coal *plus* the most
powerful short-lived warming agent we control. For home heating in 2026,
calling a wood stove climate-friendly is indefensible.
