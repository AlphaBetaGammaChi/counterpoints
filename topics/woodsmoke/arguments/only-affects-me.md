# Claim: "It's my home — my stove only affects me"

**Short answer:** Wood smoke is secondhand smoke at street scale.
Ultrafine particles drift into neighbouring gardens and infiltrate
neighbouring homes *through closed windows*, building hyper-local
hotspots that can expose the household next door to ~100× the
regional background. A private choice with a public smoke plume is
not a private choice.

## The evidence

**It is literally "the other secondhand smoke".**
Wood-smoke particles penetrate building envelopes — neighbouring homes
take in the smoke even with windows and doors shut.
→ [DSAWSP, "Wood smoke is the other secondhand smoke"](https://www.dsawsp.org/secondhand-smoke/the-other-secondhand-smoke)

**Hyper-local hotspots regulators never see.**
Fine-scale mapping in upstate New York found wood-smoke hotspots in
places no regulatory monitor covers — official readings miss where
people actually breathe.
→ Su et al., *Air Qual. Atmos. Health* (2011),
[doi:10.1007/s11869-011-0148-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11869-011-0148-1)

Neighbours of wood-burning homes can face particle levels **~100×**
what regional monitoring suggests.
→ [DSAWSP](https://www.dsawsp.org/health/wood-smoke-is-pm)

**Indoor burning becomes everyone's ambient air.**
Household solid-fuel burning is a major contributor to *outdoor* PM2.5
and the global disease burden — it doesn't stay in the flue, and once
outside it doesn't stay on your property.
→ Chafe et al., *Environ. Health Perspect.* (2014),
[doi:10.1289/ehp.1206340](https://doi.org/10.1289/ehp.1206340)

**Community-level proof:** when Launceston cut wood-heater use,
*population* mortality fell ~28% (respiratory) — benefits that accrued
to everyone breathing the shared air, not just those who swapped stoves.
→ Johnston et al., *BMJ* (2013),
[doi:10.1136/bmj.e8446](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.e8446)

**Where it's worst, children pay:** in French Alpine valleys, wood smoke
accumulating under winter inversions has forced schools to keep pupils
indoors at break times.
→ [BBC News (2017)](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-europe-39115829)

## Steelman — where the claim has a point

Your own exposure is indeed your business — and the heaviest dose from a
stove does land on the household that runs it (door-opening PM2.5
floods the living room; see
[Chakraborty 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/atmos11121326)).

## Why it still fails

- The chimney doesn't end at the property line. In terraced and
  semi-detached streets, chimney plumes discharge at neighbouring
  bedroom-window height.
- The people harmed — asthmatic children next door, an elderly
  neighbour whose windows share your smoke plume — never consented.
- Every other source of neighbourhood-scale smoke (bonfires, garden
  burning, idling engines) is already regulated or socially
  unacceptable on exactly this principle.

## Bottom line

A chimney is a smokestack. "My home, my choice" ends where the plume
crosses the fence — and the measurements show it crosses the fence,
the wall, and the neighbour's closed windows.
