title: "Rebuttal: 'Modern eco-stoves are clean'"
---

# Claim: "Modern Ecodesign / EPA-certified / Defra-exempt stoves are clean"

**Short answer:** "Cleaner than an old stove" is not "clean". The UK Chief
Medical Officer's own figures show an Ecodesign stove emits **~450× more
PM2.5 per unit of heat than a gas boiler** — and a pellet boiler ~300×.
"Clean" here means *relative to the dirtiest heating technology ever
mass-marketed* — not relative to the alternatives anyone should actually
use.

## The evidence

**The government's own numbers (EMEP 2019, via CMO 2022):**

| Appliance | PM2.5 (g/MWh) | Multiple of gas boiler (0.72 g/MWh) |
|---|---|---|
| Solid-fuel open fire | 2,950 | ~4,100× |
| Older non-exempt stove | 2,660 | ~3,700× |
| **Ecodesign / Defra-exempt stove** | **335** | **~450×** |
| Pellet-fired boiler | 216 | ~300× |
| Oil-fired boiler | 5.4 | ~7.5× |
| Gas-fired boiler | 0.72 | 1× |
| Electric heating | 0 | 0 |

→ [Chief Medical Officer's Annual Report 2022, Fig. 10](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/chief-medical-officers-annual-report-2022-air-pollution);
coverage: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2022/dec/08/eco-wood-burners-produce-450-times-more-pollution-than-gas-heating-report)

**An Ecodesign stove ≈ up to six Euro-6 HGVs.**
The Air Quality Expert Group's biomass report estimates one Ecodesign
stove can emit up to six times as much PM2.5 as a Euro-6 heavy goods
vehicle.
→ [AQEG, The Potential Air Quality Impacts from Biomass Combustion (2017)](https://uk-air.defra.gov.uk/library/reports.php?report_id=946)

**Lab certification ≠ real-world performance.**
Emission factors in real homes swing many-fold with wood moisture,
species and operation; certification tests use ideal fuel and ideal
firing cycles.
→ McDonald et al., *Environ. Sci. Technol.* (2000),
[doi:10.1021/es9909632](https://doi.org/10.1021/es9909632)

**Refuelling floods the room — certification doesn't cover it.**
Real-world monitoring of UK homes (including exempt appliances) found
PM2.5 flooding living rooms every time the door was opened to refuel.
→ Chakraborty et al., *Atmosphere* (2020),
[doi:10.3390/atmos11121326](https://doi.org/10.3390/atmos11121326)

**Combustion conditions dominate toxicity.**
Smouldering, air-starved burns — how most people actually run stoves on
a winter evening — emit more and more-toxic particles than the hot,
clean test cycle.
→ Kocbach Bølling et al., *Part. Fibre Toxicol.* (2009),
[doi:10.1186/1743-8977-6-29](https://doi.org/10.1186/1743-8977-6-29)

**US context:** EPA-certified stoves emit ~70% less PM than old
conventional stoves — a genuine improvement that still leaves them
orders of magnitude above gas, electric or heat-pump heating.
→ [EPA Burn Wise](https://www.epa.gov/burnwise)

## Steelman — where the claim has a point

Certification schemes have genuinely driven emissions *down* relative to
1980s stoves, and if a household *must* burn solid fuel, an Ecodesign
stove burning dry wood is the least-bad option. Harm reduction is real.

## Why it still fails

- "70% less than the dirtiest option" still lands hundreds of times
  above the clean options — and **infinitely above electric**.
- The certification tests a laboratory cycle, not door-openings,
  slumber-burning, wet-ish wood, or a decade of degraded seals and
  catalysts.
- Marketing has converted "meets the minimum legal bar" into "eco" —
  a claim the CMO's figures flatly contradict.

## Bottom line

There is no clean wood stove. There are dirtier and less-dirty ways to
do a fundamentally dirty thing. If the comparison is an old stove, buy
the Ecodesign; if the comparison is any modern heating system, the
Ecodesign loses by two to three orders of magnitude.
