# Campaign groups, official programmes & further resources

The science stands on its own — but these organisations translate it into
action, maintain evidence libraries, and support people affected by
neighbour wood smoke. Useful for citations *and* for pointing affected
people somewhere constructive.

## Health & science advocacy

**Doctors & Scientists Against Wood Smoke Pollution (DSAWSP)**
[dsawsp.org](https://www.dsawsp.org)
A US-based organisation of physicians and scientists; the successor
community to Utah Physicians for a Healthy Environment's wood-smoke
work. Their evidence pages are unusually well-referenced — the "wood
smoke is the other secondhand smoke" and "wood smoke is particle
pollution" briefings are among the best public explainers. Best for:
health arguments, infiltration/hotspot numbers, US regulatory context.

**Mums for Lungs**
[mumsforlungs.org](https://www.mumsforlungs.org)
UK parent-led clean-air campaign with a dedicated wood-burning work
stream; strong on the child-health framing and UK policy levers
(Smoke Control Areas, stove sales regulation).

**Global Action Plan / Clean Air Hub**
[actionforcleanair.org.uk](https://www.actionforcleanair.org.uk) ·
[cleanairhub.org.uk](https://cleanairhub.org.uk)
UK charity running Clean Air Day; publishes the *Domestic Burning FAQs*
— the most useful UK-facing myth-busting document (the "8% have no
alternative heating" figure, urban/affluent demographics of burners).

**Local clean-air groups**
Community groups (e.g. neighbourhood "clean air" alliances in Bristol,
London borough groups, US Communities for Clean Air affiliates) are
often the practical route for chimney-specific disputes — check for a
local group before reinventing one.

## Official programmes (not advocacy — but useful)

**US EPA Burn Wise** — [epa.gov/burnwise](https://www.epa.gov/burnwise)
Voluntary programme; the honest official statement that even certified
stoves emit far more than alternatives. Good for conceding-and-pivoting
("even the EPA's best case…").

**Defra Burn Better, Breathe Better / Ready to Burn**
UK government campaigns on dry fuel and correct operation — useful for
the "best practice" argument since it concedes the problem while
framing the solution as technique rather than fuel switching.

**Health Canada — Residential wood smoke**
[publications.gc.ca](https://publications.gc.ca/site/eng/9.917507/publication.html)
Strong national statistics (wood ≈ 21% of Canadian PM2.5) and
plain-language health guidance.

## Using these sources honestly

A note on credibility: in a debate, lead with the peer-reviewed
literature and official statistics — they can't be dismissed as
campaign material. Introduce advocacy groups as *where to act on the
evidence*, not as the evidence itself. The exception is DSAWSP, whose
briefings are written and referenced by clinicians and are legitimate
secondary sources.

## If you're affected by a neighbour's wood smoke

1. Document: dates, times, photos of visible smoke — patterns matter
   to environmental health officers.
2. In the UK, smoke from a chimney can constitute a **statutory
   nuisance** (Environmental Protection Act 1990); Smoke Control Area
   rules also apply in many towns — contact the local authority
   environmental health team.
3. Indoor mitigation: well-sealed windows, a HEPA air purifier in the
   rooms most affected — proven to cut indoor PM2.5 substantially.
4. Share the evidence: the [rebuttal docs](arguments/) and
   [slides](slides.html) are built to be forwarded.
